Food items can be eaten to restore your health or stamina, and some provide additional benefits to magicka, archery, block, barter, carry weight, cure disease, poison/fire/frost resistance, waterbreathing, or warmth.

Raw food provides only trivial benefits, but it can be cooked into 58 food items, 2 poisoned apples, and 17 hot soups, or baked into 15 food items, to increase the benefits. Food is cheaper and more widely available than potions, and creating cooked food does not involve the Alchemy skill. However, even cooked food typically provides weaker effects than potions do. Nonetheless, a small number of dishes provide quite long-lasting effects, with several of the soups, stews, and bisques like Beef Stew lasting longer than all stamina potions, and Elsweyr Fondue is actually more powerful than a number of magicka potions.

Stamina regeneration food such as vegetable soup is rather useful for melee based characters; the stamina regeneration each second allows you to use power attacks continuously until the effect wears off. Alcoholic beverages like ale, mead, and wine typically provide an immediate boost to stamina but reduced stamina regeneration for a period of time.

Most food items can be sold to any innkeeper or food vendor, as well as to general goods merchants (exceptions are noted in the following tables). A limited number of food items are categorized as raw food, and these items can also be sold to apothecary merchants. In addition, innkeepers and food vendors will have a selection of food items available for purchase.

With the addition of Survival Mode food is also used to replenish Hunger. Different foods have different weights of replenishment lowering the Hunger meter by set amounts. Very small replenishes 2, small replenishes 18, medium replenishes 220, and large replenishes 380.

Foods added by Dawnguard, Dragonborn, Hearthfire, and Creation Club are annotated with "DG", "DB", "HF", and "CC" superscripts, respectively.

Regular Food[edit]

This section includes all food items that cannot be created by the player by baking or cooking, although most of these items can be used as ingredients in baking and cooking recipes. Several of these items are also classified by merchants as being raw food; they can be sold to apothecary merchants and innkeepers.

Raw Meat[edit]

Raw meat can generally be obtained from the bodies of dead animals. In the case of small game (e.g., rabbits and pheasants), already-caught animals can also be found hanging in homes and inns, and can be harvested to obtain the meat. Similarly, salmon can also be found hanging from racks near rivers. A variety of fish can be caught by hand or by using a rod with the Fishing Creation. Certain barrels are internally identified as meat barrels and will always contain raw meat, as well as salt piles and other ingredients.

Three stall merchants, Hogni Red-Arm in Markarth, Anoriath in Whiterun and Marise Aravel in Riften, specialize in raw meat. In addition, some varieties of meat can be bought from hunters encountered in the wilderness.

Nearly all raw meat can be cooked by simply combining it with salt. A few meats (beef, chicken, clams, crab, horker, venison) can also be made into more elaborate dishes or stews by combining them with vegetables as well as salt.

Beverages[edit]

Consuming alcoholic beverages like meads, wines, and brandies typically provide an immediate boost to stamina, along with reduced stamina regeneration, for a period of time.

Several other alcoholic beverages are classified as potions instead of food, meaning they are displayed in your inventory under potions:

  • Argonian Ale
  • Balmora Blue wine
  • Cliff Racer wine
  • Cyrodilic Brandy
  • Skooma, including Double-Distilled Skooma and Kordir's Skooma
  • Stros M'Kai Rum
  • Velvet LeChance wine
  • White-Gold Tower wine

Many of these other beverages do not have the negative reduced stamina regeneration side effect.

Notes[edit]

  • During A Night To Remember, only the Alto Wine variant with the basket on the bottom counts as a quest item; the one that lacks it doesn't. Likewise, regular wine, Spiced Wine, and empty wine bottles also do not count.
  • Your inventory says that cabbages, eidar cheese wedges, goat cheese wedges, and Sadri's Sujamma weigh 0.3 instead of 0.25. It is unknown if this is normal or a bug.
  • Version 2.0.9 of the Unofficial Skyrim Patch makes it possible to sell a good majority of the original player-cooked foods to food and drink vendors, as well as Firebrand Wine and Colovian Brandy.
    • Version 2.0.9 of the Unofficial Dragonborn Patch also makes it possible to sell Horker and Ash Yam Stew to food and drink vendors, as well as some of the food exclusively found on Solstheim, such as Boar Meat.
      • Version 1.0.1 of the Unofficial Hearthfire Patch makes it possible to sell Potato Soup and Clam Chowder to food and drink vendors.

Bugs[edit]

  • Vegetable Soup and Venison Stew may not restore health properly.
    • The Official Skyrim Special Edition Patch, version 1.5.73, fixes this bug.
  • Seared Slaughterfish and Charred Skeever Meat may restore less Hunger than intended.
    • The Official Skyrim Special Edition Patch, version 1.6, fixes this bug.
  • Some food and drink items may not make the proper sounds when picked up, dropped, and/or consumed.
    • The Official Skyrim Special Edition Patch, version 1.6, fixes this bug.
  • Flin, Mazte, Shein, Sujamma, Sadri's Sujamma, and Emberbrand Wine all lack proper pickup and drop sounds.
    • The Unofficial Skyrim Legendary Edition Patch, version 3.0.8, fixes this bug.
  • Boar Meat, Ash Hopper Meat, Ash Hopper Leg, and Cooked Boar Meat all display 0 value due to not having set the no-autocalc flag on them.
    • The Unofficial Skyrim Legendary Edition Patch, version 3.0.8, fixes this bug.
  • Horker and Ash Yam Stew doesn't use the same eating sounds used by every other stew object in the game.
    • The Unofficial Skyrim Special Edition Patch, version 4.2.5, fixes this bug.
